What we believe

Six rules.
Every screen follows them.

These are design decisions you can see in the product. They decide what gets built and what gets left out.

A clinical cockpit.

Dense, calm and quick to scan. Each case gets a few minutes at the board, so the screen shows what the decision needs and little else.

Speed over spectacle.

Motion in the app settles in about 220 milliseconds. Nothing decorative stands between a clinician and the next case.

Colour means something.

Every organ board has its own hue. Rose, amber and emerald carry urgency and status, always beside a text label, so nobody relies on colour alone.

Data is monospace.

MRNs, TNM, variants, millimetres and HU are set in a monospaced face, so columns line up and a 1 never passes for an l.

De-identified by default.

An MRN and initials identify each case. The case record has no field for a full name.

AI drafts. Clinicians decide.

Assistants are labelled, disclaimed and run only when asked. A clinician casts every vote and signs every decision.

Lines we hold

What we will
never do.

Some decisions are settled before any roadmap discussion starts.

  • Store a patient's full name.

    The case record has no field for one. Cases are identified by MRN and initials, and that will not change.

  • Let AI vote, sign off or change the record.

    Assistants read and draft. Every vote, every sign-off and every edit is made by a clinician under their own account.

  • Hide who voted for what.

    Each vote is stored with the specialty and clinician who cast it, and dissent stays with the case.

  • Animate the diagnostic image.

    Motion stays in the controls around the DICOM viewer. The image moves only when a clinician moves it.
